Diego Jorrat

Assistant Professor, Department of Applied Economics I, Universidad de Sevilla
Researcher, Loyola Behavioral Lab · Data Editor, Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Economics

I am an experimental and behavioral economist. I run experiments in the lab, online and in the field to study how people decide when outcomes depend on others, and how incentives, information and uncertainty shape those decisions. Much of my work measures economic preferences and strategic reasoning in schools and in development settings, in Spain, Honduras and Nigeria.

I hold a Ph.D. in Data Science from Universidad Loyola Andalucía (2021) and an M.Sc. in Economics from Universidad de San Andrés. Before my doctorate I directed the Impact Evaluation Unit of the Buenos Aires City Government.

Working papers

  1. 2026
    Strategic uncertainty in collective action problems
    with P. Brañas-Garza, A. Cabrales and M. P. Espinosa
    In a public goods game with a representative sample of 1,500 Spanish adults, ambiguity about the game's parameters does not affect contributions. Strategic uncertainty about others' behavior does.
  2. 2026
    Time Blocking, Goal Setting, and Work Performance
    with M. Drouvelis, J. Gómez-Miñambres and P. Kujal
    Dividing work into short blocks raises attempts by about 30% but lowers accuracy. Non-binding goals help only high-ability workers.

    Coordination with and without incentives
